ExpFrame was constructed as an interpretable post-prediction prioritization work-
flow to connect computational AMP discovery and generation with experimental
peptide selection. The input comprised high-confidence peptides identified by AMP P
from habitat-specific smORF pools and peptides generated by the corresponding
AMP G models. Candidate sequences were first filtered to retain peptides composed of
standard amino acids, and redundant entries were removed before cross-comparison.
ExpFrame then assigned candidates to three evidence classes. In the sequence-
similarity module, predicted and generated candidate sets were compared with
MMseqs2 [63]; pairs with an E-value < 1 × 10−3 were retained as sequence-similarity
analogues. In the structure-similarity module, peptide structures were predicted with
ESMFold [16] and searched against the positive AMP reference-structure set with Fold-
seek [64]; candidates with structural alignments passing TM-score > 0.5 were retained
as structure-similarity analogues. For generated peptides not retained by either simi-
larity module, 1,280-dimensional ESM-2-650M fixed-length sequence embeddings from
the model’s leading [CLS] special token were extracted and compared with the cen-
troid and nearest-neighbour distribution of the positive AMP reference set. Generated
peptides that remained close to the AMP embedding manifold while lacking sequence
or structure-level matches were retained as divergent-generation candidates.
